Scenario 1: Health & Fitness Mobile App — “FitLife”
1. Identifying and Writing Problem Statements
Overview:

The goal of “FitLife” is to help users maintain a consistent fitness routine by tracking workouts, setting achievable goals, and visualizing progress through a simple, motivating, and clutter-free interface.

User Pain Points:

Inconsistent fitness routines due to lack of motivation.

Overly complex app features create confusion.

Lack of visual progress tracking discourages continued use.

Notifications are not customized to user habits.

Beginners feel overwhelmed by technical workout data.

Problem Statements:

Primary Problem:
“Busy working professionals need a way to track their daily workouts and progress because their tight schedules make it hard to stay consistent, which results in poor fitness habits and decreased motivation.”

Secondary Problem:
“Beginner fitness users need a simple way to visualize their fitness journey because complicated charts and data discourage them, which results in abandoning fitness goals.”

2. Identifying Appropriate Research Methods
a. Surveys

Purpose: Gather quantitative data about user fitness habits and preferred features.

Execution: Conducted via online Google Forms distributed in colleges, gyms, and social media fitness groups.

Sample Questions:

How many days per week do you exercise?

What features do you value most in a fitness app?

What keeps you motivated to continue working out?

b. One-on-One Interviews

Purpose: Understand emotional triggers, motivations, and frustrations.

Execution: 8–10 semi-structured interviews (20–30 minutes each).

Sample Questions:

What frustrates you when tracking workouts?

How do you stay motivated to exercise regularly?

c. Usability Testing (Prototype Phase)

Purpose: Observe how users perform key actions using a wireframe or prototype.

Metrics Observed: Task completion time, success rate, and user satisfaction levels.

Justification:

Combining surveys for broad insights, interviews for emotional depth, and usability testing for direct observation ensures a data-driven and empathetic design approach.

🛍️ Scenario 2: E-Commerce Platform — “StyleEase”
1. Identifying and Writing Problem Statements
Overview:

“StyleEase” is an e-commerce fashion platform that helps users find well-fitting, high-quality clothing easily, with quick checkout and minimal hassle.

User Pain Points:

Confusing size charts that differ across brands.

Product images often look different from reality.

Return and refund processes are slow.

Overwhelming product variety.

Time wasted reordering favorite products.

Problem Statements:

Primary Problem:
“Online shoppers need a way to find clothes that fit their size accurately because size charts vary across brands, which results in frequent returns and frustration.”

Secondary Problem:
“Returning customers need a quick way to reorder favorite items because re-browsing wastes time, which reduces loyalty and satisfaction.”

2. Identifying Appropriate Research Methods
a. User Interviews

Understand pain points related to fit, returns, and product trust.

Ask about online shopping confidence and frustrations.

b. Card Sorting

Improve product categorization by grouping clothing types as per user perception.

Result: More intuitive navigation and faster discovery.

c. A/B Testing

Compare two designs (e.g., product detail layouts, “Try-On” AR feature).

Measure performance through click-through rate, add-to-cart rate, and checkout completion.

Justification:

User interviews provide emotional understanding, card sorting improves usability, and A/B testing validates real design performance — creating an experience that builds trust and efficiency.
